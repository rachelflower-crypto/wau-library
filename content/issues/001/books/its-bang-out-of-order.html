It was 9.04 on a Tuesday when the facilitator said the words. “Before we start, let’s go round the room and share a fun fact about ourselves.”

I have sat through a great deal in my professional life. Restructures. Away days. A two-hour workshop on the new expenses system that ended, I promise you, in a song. But nothing, nothing, makes a room of competent adults wilt like being asked to be fun on cue.

The song was about receipts.

Watch it happen. Shoulders rise. Eyes drop to the table. Somebody near the front, who has been dreading this since the calendar invite arrived, quietly rehearses “I’ve been to Belgium twice” like a hostage reading a statement.

And the women. Oh, the women. We know exactly how this game is scored. Too clever and you’re showing off. Too personal and you’re oversharing. Too modest and you’ve “undersold yourself”, which will come up again at your appraisal. So we pick something charming and safe, like a cardigan.

Nobody has ever been made to feel safe by being made to perform safety.

Two Truths and a Lie is worse. It asks you to lie at work, in front of your manager, in the name of team cohesion. Then everyone guesses which bit of you is false. For a good many women, that’s just called Tuesday.

I’m not against warmth. I’m against warmth with a stopwatch. If you want people to talk to each other, give them something real to talk about and a decent biscuit, and then get out of the way.

The History is a man. He’s allowed in by special permission, and we make him do all the graft. He loves it.

So here is my modest proposal. Next time someone asks you for a fun fact, you are within your rights to say: “I’m a delight once I’ve had my coffee. Ask me again at eleven.” Then smile. Then say nothing more at all.

Tested. Works.

Or, if you’re feeling braver: “My fun fact is that my name is [insert name] and I like biscuits.”

It isn’t rude. It’s a boundary with good manners. Which, if you ask me, is the most fun fact there is.

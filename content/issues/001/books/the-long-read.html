There is a box in my loft labelled MISC that contains, as far as I can tell, four previous versions of me.

There’s the nineteen-year-old with the unfortunate fringe and the absolute certainty. There’s the one in the borrowed suit who said yes to everything, because she thought that was what keen looked like. There’s the one who went very quiet for a while. And there’s the one who started, slowly, to find her voice again, and then rather enjoyed using it.

For years I treated that box like evidence. Proof of wrong turns. Every so often I’d open it, wince, and close it again, the way you might skim an old email you sent in a temper.

We are awfully good at this, aren’t we? Looking back at our earlier selves with the cold eye of an auditor. She should have left sooner. She should have spoken up. She should never, ever have worn that.

Especially that.
What if the women we’ve been weren’t mistakes to be corrected, but founders?

Here is what I’ve come to believe. Each of those women built something. Not always something beautiful. Sometimes it was a wall, put up in a hurry because the weather was bad. But she built it with the materials she had, in the conditions she was in, and it kept her standing long enough to become me.

Refounding is the word I’ve settled on for what happens next. Not starting again: you can’t, and you shouldn’t want to. Not “reinventing yourself”, which always sounds exhausting and slightly like a product launch. Refounding is going back down to the foundations with a lantern and a bit of curiosity. What’s still holding? What was only ever scaffolding? What can be let go of, with thanks?

Scaffolding is allowed to come down. That’s what it’s for.
THE HISTORY NOTESFound, founder and foundation share a root: the Latin fundus, the bottom of a thing. To found something is to go all the way down.

It turns out you can be grateful to a version of yourself without agreeing with her. I’m grateful to the woman in the borrowed suit. She was frightened and she went anyway. She just didn’t need to go everywhere.

And the women we shall become? I don’t think we’re required to have them planned. I’ve stopped believing in the five-year version of me who rises at five and journals. I’d rather leave her some room. A clear shelf. A door slightly ajar.

So this month, an invitation rather than a resolution. Go up to the loft, real or otherwise. Open the box. Say hello to whoever’s in there. You don’t have to forgive everything, and you don’t have to explain anything. You only have to stop treating her as a stranger.

She got you here, after all. That’s not nothing. That’s a foundation.

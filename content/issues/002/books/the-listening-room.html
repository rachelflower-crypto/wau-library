There is something extraordinary about Eva Cassidy’s voice.

Its intimacy. Its restraint. The way she seems to inhabit every word rather than merely sing it.

Her recording of Fields of Gold, written by Sting, is a song about love, memory and the passage of time.

The fields are golden, not fallow.

But perhaps that is precisely why the song belongs here.

It reminds us that the landscape of our lives is never fixed.

There are seasons of abundance and seasons of emptiness. Moments we wish we could hold forever, and moments we struggle to endure.

And sometimes we only understand the significance of a season when we look back upon it.

Cassidy’s own story is a strange, sad lesson in exactly that. She recorded the song live at Blues Alley, a jazz club in Washington DC, in January 1996. She died later that year, aged 33, largely unknown outside her home town.

In 1998 her recordings were gathered into an album, Songbird, which opened with Fields of Gold. It sat in near-obscurity for two years. Then Radio 2 started playing her: Mike Harding, and Terry Wogan, whose producer Paul Walters had passed him the CD. In March 2001, Songbird reached number one in the UK.

Thank you, Terry.

For years, that voice lay fallow. Nobody was listening. And then, in a season she never saw, it found its harvest.

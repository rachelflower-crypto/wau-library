There are books that arrive at exactly the right time.

Katherine May’s Wintering: The Power of Rest and Retreat in Difficult Times (Rider, 2020) is one of those books worth keeping close when life feels out of season.

May explores the difficult winters of human experience: illness, loss, exhaustion, withdrawal and the unexpected periods when our ordinary lives become unfamiliar. The book grew out of her own: her husband became seriously ill, and then so did she.

Rather than presenting these seasons as personal failures to overcome, she considers what the natural world and the cultures of colder places might teach us about inhabiting them. Dormice. Solstice. Icy swims. Arctic seas.

And here’s the moment I knew she belonged in this issue. May describes wintering as “a fallow period in life.” Her word, not mine.

I may have squeaked.

The central proposition is both simple and challenging.

Winter is part of life.

Not an aberration. Not necessarily a catastrophe. Not evidence that we’ve somehow taken a wrong turn.

And if we cannot always prevent winter from arriving, perhaps we can learn to live within it.

Note, too, that May’s winters are rarely chosen. They arrive. That matters, because it means wintering isn’t a lifestyle trend for people with spare time. It’s something that happens to all of us, ready or not.

For We Are Unbecoming, the question is not simply how to get through winter.

It’s whether we can stop treating winter as something we ought to be ashamed of.
